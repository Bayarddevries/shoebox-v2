# Consent FAQ — for MMF/RRMNHC employees

> Internal reference. For questions from citizens about the Shoebox Project's
> consent form, point them to metisshoebox@mmf.mb.ca or (204) 295-3467.
> This FAQ explains what the operative form (V3, "MMF Red River Métis Shoebox
> Project Consent, Release and Copyright Licence Agreement") covers.

---

## What is this form?

A legal agreement a citizen signs when they contribute photos to the Shoebox
Project. It gives MMF permission to use their photos and tells the citizen
exactly what MMF may and may not do with them.

## What does MMF get the right to do with the photos?

Under the agreement, the citizen grants MMF an unlimited, non-exclusive,
worldwide, royalty-free, fully-paid licence to all copyright they have in the
photos. In plain language, MMF may:

- Digitize, reproduce, and archive the photos
- Display and exhibit them to the public, including online, on social media,
  and at the Red River Métis National Heritage Centre
- Include them in MMF publications
- Modify, arrange, and create derivative works (e.g. crops, framing, exhibit
  layouts)

This licence is **perpetual** (it does not expire).

## Who owns the photos after signing?

The citizen keeps the copyright. They are licensing (giving permission for)
MMF's use, not transferring ownership. MMF holds a licence, not ownership.
They also keep the physical photographs.

## What does "licence" mean? Is that different from ownership?

Yes. Ownership = you own the copyright and control who uses it. Licence =
you give someone else permission to use it, while you keep ownership. Think
of it like lending a book to a library: the library can display it, but it's
still your book.

## Can a citizen still use or share their own photos?

Yes. The licence is non-exclusive, which means it does not take away the
citizen's own rights. They can keep using, sharing, and printing their own
photos.

## Can a citizen change their mind later?

Yes, with one important limit. They may terminate the agreement at any time
by emailing metisshoebox@mmf.mb.ca. The termination:

- Does not apply retroactively (uses that already happened are allowed to stay)
- Does not apply to copies already physically printed in MMF publications or
  in the hands of third parties

So a citizen can stop NEW uses of their photos, but cannot force MMF to
un-publish something already printed or pull copies already distributed.

## What is the USB checkbox on the form?

The form asks whether the citizen wants to receive digitized copies of their
photos on a USB drive. It's their choice. The USB is part of the "good and
valuable consideration" the form cites, but the form also cites "other good
and valuable consideration", so declining the USB does not void the licence.

## What did the citizen agree to about their name and likeness?

They consented to MMF using their name and likeness (as shown in the photos)
in association with the Shoebox Project, including on websites and social
media.

## Can MMF share citizen contact information?

Yes, with the citizen's authorization in the form: MMF may share their name
and contact information with a third party, such as a researcher, who would
like to request permission to use or reproduce their photos. That is
researcher-driven contact, not unsolicited sharing.

## What about people who appear IN the photos but did not sign?

This is the biggest limitation of the form. It covers the **donor's** rights
and consent. It does not obtain consent from other identifiable people shown
in the photos. Those individuals retain their own privacy and personality
rights. Practical rule: if a photo shows identifiable people who are not the
donor and may not have consented, treat it as sensitive, and review before
public display. Flag such photos rather than assuming the donor's signature
covers everyone in the frame.

## What if there is no signed form?

For new intake: do not ingest or publicly display the photos until consent
is on file. One signed form covers all the photos listed on it for that
contributor, but the form must exist and be linked in the consent register
first. Note: a legacy backlog of ~107 photos (from early intake before the
register existed) is currently displayed without documented consent; the
project is clearing those via retro-consent follow-ups, and any photo that
cannot be cleared is reviewed for removal rather than assumed covered.

## What about moral rights?

The citizen waives their moral rights (the right to be credited and the right
to object to changes) in favour of MMF. This is standard and protects MMF
when it crops, frames, or adapts photos for exhibits.

## Where do signed forms live?

Digitized PDFs are kept with the project files; the consent register (a
per-photo record of consent status) tracks coverage. See
`scripts/consent-register-import.csv` and the Consent Register tab of the
Shoebox Submitter Metadata sheet.

## Quick reference — can MMF...?

| Action | Yes/No |
|---|---|
| Display photos publicly, online, on social media | Yes (with signed form) |
| Exhibit photos at the Heritage Centre | Yes |
| Use photos in MMF publications | Yes |
| Print/crop/adapt photos for exhibits | Yes (moral rights waived) |
| Claim ownership/copyright | No — MMF holds a licence, citizen keeps copyright |
| Stop new uses after citizen termination | Yes |
| Share citizen contact with researchers | Yes (authorized in the form) |
| Display photos with unconsented people in frame | Review first — flag as sensitive |
| Publish without any signed form | No |
